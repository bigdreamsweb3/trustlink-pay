TIN Identity and Verified Credentials in the Internet of Value

A ten-digit Transfer Identity Number (TIN) gives a sender a human-facing recipient identifier. TIP, the Transfer Identity Protocol, manages registration, authenticated route membership and owner recovery. The Node resolves the permitted recipient route after the sender signs a TIN-targeted intent, while the sender's wallet owner separately approves funding. A TIN is neither a private key nor proof of legal identity.

Identity-addressed value

Transfer Settlement Network (TSN) is hybrid decentralized settlement protocol infrastructure for the Internet of Value. It combines off-chain verification and coordination with on-chain authorization, private-credit proofs and reserve accounting. This separation lets applications use a payment identity without turning that identity into spending authority.

Current recipient-name boundary

Current identity lookup resolves an owner-provided name and returns an empty verifiedCredentials collection. A credential-shaped signed schema is not issuer authentication or verification of the name. Applications must describe the current display name separately from any future provider-verified credential.

A proposed credential extension

A future owner-consented attestation could associate a provider-verified personal or business name with a TIN. A payment application could then show a permitted verified name during recipient confirmation and explain who issued it. The goal is more informed recipient selection, rather than an automatic assurance that every identity or payment is safe.

Required verification and disclosure rules

  • Validate a supported issuer and credential schema, including the exact claim being made.
  • Bind the credential to the intended TIN and owner-consent flow rather than relying on a display name.
  • Check expiry and revocation before showing the credential as valid.
  • Disclose only the claim needed for recipient confirmation and retain clear privacy policy.
  • Keep payment authorization, wallet-control proof and legal-identity verification as distinct checks.

Current implementation boundary

No integrated Solana Attestation Service or Sumsub credential flow is established by the inspected TSN implementation. These are potential ecosystem references, not announced TSN partners or verified product features. The experimental Solana private-credit flow and its retained Devnet checkpoint do not establish credential interoperability or complete public-observer unlinkability.

Developer and merchant use cases

Wallet developers can already design around the current experimental TIN-targeted approval and settlement lifecycle. A verified merchant-name display remains a future credential use case requiring issuer, policy and owner-consent integration. New settlement networks also need route-specific value delivery and recovery; an attested identity alone cannot settle assets across chains.
