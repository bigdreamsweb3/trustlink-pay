---
title: "How GPRU Proofs Guard User State"
description: "A plain-language overview of the zero-knowledge proof intended to protect private state before TCAP settlement."
---

The experimental private-credit replacement keeps thirteen public inputs and a V2 verifier with fourteen IC points, but needs newly matching circuit keys. Existing V2 amount-public keys require explicit rekey; V1 accounts require their separate one-time layout migration. Complete epoch accounting has a separate five-input verifier/key and six IC points. [Operator setup](/operators/run-your-own-tsn) distinguishes deployment, funding-root initialization, credit rekey, epoch enrollment and persistent service configuration.

GPRU is designed to guard the integrity and privacy of a user's private state as it is presented to TCAP for a balance-changing action. A Groth16 proof lets a verifier check a statement about hidden data without receiving the private data itself.

In this design, the proof is intended to establish that the user's hidden state is current and that the requested change follows from it. TCAP then applies the asset, authorization, and liquidity checks and governs the actual settlement. The proof does not give GPRU custody of the funds or independently authorize a payment.

For native credit, the SDK generates a private funding-note opening and the wallet signs its commitment. After checking the actual deposit transfer, TCAP appends a leaf bound to that commitment, registered token and amount. The circuit privately reconstructs the leaf, proves membership under the released sealed funding root, binds the exact asset/amount to the credit and derives its one-time nullifier from the note secret. Reusing that secret cannot produce a second nullifier by changing the Node, work ID or root. The opening, leaf, note commitment and append index are absent from the public credit statement.

The proof is one part of a larger protocol contract: the private statement, transaction record, on-chain verifier, and TCAP state transition must all agree. A proof that checks only a different statement would not validate the user's encrypted balance record.

The current experimental credit publishes a freshly blinded amount commitment instead of plaintext amount, and advances the head/nullifier root plus epoch digest without reserve liability changes. Node prepares a separate proof for every ordered opening in a 2–64-credit epoch; TCAP/GPRU reclassify that authenticated total once. Sealed positive-funding pools survive later deposits and use common release windows. Deposits and epoch totals remain public, as do token, roots, points, ciphertext, accounts and timing. Registry/head history and Node knowledge can still correlate payments. Offline real proofs passed; SBF build, activation and complete live accounting remain unverified.

The circuit and proving-key lifecycle are engineering materials kept in the [repository documentation](https://github.com/bigdreamsweb3/trustlink-pay/tree/main/docs), rather than setup instructions for an application integrator.

The public funding root is also an anonymity-set boundary. The November 5 public-only regression reproduced both old signature/nullifier links and found that the new nullifier no longer follows that formula, while linking the new credit to its only deposit through an independently reconstructed one-leaf funding root. A successful proof over one eligible deposit does not conceal which deposit funded it. Larger shared-root sets require separate analysis of public token/amount, timing and prior spend knowledge; see [the threat model](/security/threat-model).
