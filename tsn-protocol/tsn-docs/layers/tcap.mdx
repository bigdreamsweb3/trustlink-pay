**TCAP (Transfer Confidential Asset Protocol)** governs the assets and liquidity used for TSN settlement. It checks that an asset is accepted, that the action has the required authorization, and that the applicable reserve conditions are met before applying a balance-changing transition.

For a private-state transition, **GPRU guards the integrity and privacy of the user's state presented to TCAP**. Its proof is intended to show that the hidden state is current and that the proposed change follows from it. TCAP remains the authority over funds and applies the valid, authorized transition atomically; GPRU does not hold the user's assets.

## How reserve protection works

TCAP tracks actual custody assets and recognized obligations for each accepted token. A public wallet deposit increases assets and pending liabilities and appends an exact amount/asset-bound funded leaf. An individual private credit consumes its note nullifier and advances the recipient state plus blinded epoch transcript, with no token movement or public liability reclassification.

TCAP checks asset policy, released sealed funding pool, current private root/sequence and reserve solvency. GPRU proves exact funded membership and recipient arithmetic for the privately committed amount. Accepted root and epoch-digest updates are atomic. Separate complete 2–64-credit epoch proof authenticates all ordered openings and moves only their total from pending to settled confidential liabilities; custody assets, total obligations and uncredited pending funding remain unchanged. Failed proof, insufficient pending, overflow or replay rejects accounting.

Aggregate solvency protects the reserve as a whole, but it cannot by itself prove that one user's private balance is correct. GPRU's proof must bind the exact asset, action, amount, current hidden state, and valid successor, including affordability for a debit or exit. TCAP must apply that same proven amount to its accounting. This is why a proof is not a fund authorization on its own and why the proof statement and TCAP transition must agree.

The reserve is an asset-level liability ledger, while encrypted owner actions live in Memo and recipient state uses a shared authenticated root. For example, deposits totaling 100 USDC can leave 20 USDC uncredited pending and 80 USDC accounted after a complete multi-credit epoch. The 80 USDC aggregate is public; individual credit amounts are blinded. Native credit epochs are distinct from retained TSNP Epoch Treasury/payout APIs.

The replacement native-credit source uses `deposit_private_funding_v1`: after verifying the exact vault delta, TCAP appends a funding leaf bound to the wallet-signed note commitment, registered token and observed amount atomically with deposit accounting. The circuit proves private membership, asset/amount binding and a secret-derived nullifier under the shared funding root. Aggregate liabilities therefore do not substitute for proof of this credit's funded note. The [proof guide](/developers/groth16-hidden-head) separates local verification from the required program/root/V2-key activation.

## Balance history

The design records encrypted action data in transaction Memo instructions so the owner can reconstruct their history from chain data. A Memo is transaction data, not a private account or a guarantee that the transition is valid. TCAP's on-chain checks and the proof must validate the action before it changes protocol state.

The public SDK `loadTinOwnerBalanceV1` performs identity authorization, registry validation and local wallet-authorized history recovery, with governed token ID to mint/decimals mapping. The `protocol-tests/ui` balance control supplies transport and renders that result. They reject incomplete or unsupported history and do not add spending or payout authority. See [XP balance troubleshooting](/sdk/troubleshooting#xp-balance-is-empty-after-a-finalized-credit) for the user flow and recovery limits.

## Current capability boundary

The private-credit replacement is experimental. Local real credit/epoch proofs and native verifier tests passed; SBF compilation, activation and complete live accounting remain unverified. Individual amount is committed, while deposits, aggregate totals, token, root progression, points, ciphertext and timing remain observable. [Threat model](/security/threat-model) records inference and Node-knowledge limits. Confidential debit/exit and liquidity-provider admission/rewards require separate work.

Pool capture requires two positive unspent member notes and two wallet sources; zero-value actors do not qualify. Release windows adapt to qualified traffic, while sparse accounting epochs carry forward rather than publishing singleton totals. These policies do not guarantee latency or independent people. [Operator activation](/operators/run-your-own-tsn) records separate credit rekey, epoch enrollment and protected opening-journal requirements.

The full TCAP instruction contract and integration decisions are maintained in the [repository documentation](https://github.com/bigdreamsweb3/trustlink-pay/tree/main/docs).
