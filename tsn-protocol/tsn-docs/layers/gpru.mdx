**GPRU (Guarded Privacy Routing Unit)** guards the integrity and privacy of a user's private state as that state is presented to TCAP. For a balance-changing action, its proof is intended to show that the hidden state is current and that the proposed transition follows from it.

This gives GPRU a verification role, not custody of funds. **TCAP governs accepted assets, authorization, reserves, and liquidity**, and applies a valid transition atomically. A GPRU proof by itself cannot move tokens or authorize a payment.

## What the proof is intended to establish

- The submitted private state is the current state for the action.
- The proposed change follows from that state and the authorized action.
- A native credit consumes a privately opened funded note whose membership, registered asset, exact amount and secret-derived nullifier are constrained by the circuit.
- The private identity and state witness need not be disclosed in plaintext to the verifier.

Experimental credit uses thirteen public inputs with a TCAP-authenticated current or retained funding root last, secret-derived nullifier and private-amount commitment binding. A new circuit requires newly matching keys despite retaining the existing verifier account layout. Separate five-input epoch verification authenticates all 2–64 accepted credit openings; TCAP owns the transcript and applies aggregate liability accounting after proof acceptance. GPRU neither schedules the epoch nor closes its account. The [TCAP lifecycle](/layers/tcap#native-credit-epoch-lifecycle) explains account reuse and Node's maturity threshold, while the [proof guide](/developers/groth16-hidden-head) separates offline proof tests from dated Devnet evidence.

## Identity privacy and public-chain visibility

The native-credit ABI hides the recipient route/head witness and does not encode a plaintext TIN or private predecessor pointer. The owner retains its record lineage. The privacy target is to hide TIN participation from public observers and prevent action linkage; trusted Node access to verification routes/openings is accepted. Shared roots, public statement fields, encrypted Memo bytes, accounts, timing and registration history can still permit recipient-registry and cross-asset correlations under known-genesis or maintained-history assumptions. The latest two real credits were not matched to owners. Confidential debit/exit remains a separate unverified execution path; see [the threat model](/security/threat-model).

For the cryptographic contract, circuit artifacts, and integration decisions, see the [GPRU documentation in the repository](https://github.com/bigdreamsweb3/trustlink-pay/tree/main/docs).
