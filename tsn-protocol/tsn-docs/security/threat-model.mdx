TSN separates user authorization, service verification, private-state protection, and settlement authority. The sender's wallet authorizes the requested payment. TSN services verify and route work. GPRU is designed to guard the integrity and privacy of the private state presented to TCAP. TCAP governs accepted assets, liquidity, and atomic settlement.

The primary privacy target is protection from public observers: avoid exposing which TIN participates in a TCAP action or letting observers group actions as belonging to the same TIN, including intended TIN-to-TIN transfers. Trusted Node access to the routes, amounts and openings needed for verification is an accepted boundary. Public wallet funding for the mobile-money flow is also accepted; its visibility must be distinguished from public linkage between later private TCAP actions.

## Participant boundaries

| Participant | Role | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sender wallet | Reviews and authorizes the payment. | A service or Cranker cannot replace the sender's approval. |
| TSN services | Coordinate and verify payment work. | Service acceptance or verification is not on-chain settlement. |
| Cranker | Submits authorized work and pays transaction fees. | Submission does not prove confirmation or give authority to alter payment terms. |
| GPRU | Guards the user's private state presented to TCAP. | A proof does not hold funds or replace TCAP checks. |
| TCAP | Governs accepted assets and applies authorized settlement. | The transition must satisfy the applicable asset, authorization, and liquidity rules. |

## Identity privacy and public-chain visibility

The native-credit ABI hides the recipient route/head witness and does not encode a plaintext TIN or private predecessor pointer. The owner retains its record lineage. Shared roots, public statement fields, encrypted Memo bytes, accounts and timing remain observable. Those fields and public registration history can still support action correlation; omitting the plaintext TIN does not prove public-observer unlinkability. Confidential debit/exit remains a separate unverified execution path.

The funded-note source derives the public nullifier from a private nonzero note secret with a domain-separated Poseidon hash. TCAP commits the registered asset and observed transfer amount in an append-only funding leaf; the circuit privately proves its membership and exact credit binding before consuming the nullifier. The credit statement excludes the note commitment, funded leaf/index and opening, removing the old public signature-to-nullifier calculation. Node receives the restricted opening and routing witness as part of trusted verification. Public epoch totals, timing and registry/head correlations still require public-observer analysis.

TIP registration publicly associates a registry with its registration owner key. The registry's `owner_commitment` is unsalted SHA-256 of that key; the owner-recovery account stores the raw key and uses it in its PDA derivation, while creation data and Ed25519 owner approval expose it in transaction history. The encrypted ten-digit TIN is a different field. A Cranker transaction payer is a separate actor and must not be assumed to be that owner. A later TCAP-action-to-registry link can therefore connect to public ownership material without decrypting the TIN.

The existing global funding account authenticates current and bounded retained root/count pairs. A single confirmed funded note supports credit without a pool account or source-count wait. Later deposits preserve membership while the selected root remains retained; latest shared head/nullifier state still prevents replay. Credits remain serialized by that spending state. Signed uncertain attempts require reconciliation before replacement; unsigned stale work is reverified. Matching credit/epoch keys and programs require operator activation.

## Capability status

A later read-only check of the first finalized native credit reconstructed its successor global root from a public TIP registry's route-key commitment and public credit fields, starting from its verified empty initial maps. This identifies a persistent public recipient registry without decrypting its ten-digit TIN. Offline controls also reproduced later same-registry and cross-asset portfolio correlations under known-history assumptions. Hiding fields from proof inputs does not establish unlinkability when their commitment can be reconstructed from enumerable public values. The [source-backed investigation](https://github.com/bigdreamsweb3/trustlink-pay/blob/main/docs/laboratory/experiments/EXP-20261103-native-credit-linkability.md) gives the bounded evidence and remaining checks.

Current-source synthetic controls again reproduce recipient-registry dictionary and cross-asset correlations under known-genesis or maintained-history assumptions. These structural tests do not match the two November 16 credits to real TIN owners; that public-observer check was not performed. The [November 16 checkpoint](https://github.com/bigdreamsweb3/trustlink-pay/blob/main/docs/laboratory/experiments/EXP-20261116-private-credit-epoch.md) separates those controls from its finalized execution receipts.

The [November 16 Devnet record](https://github.com/bigdreamsweb3/trustlink-pay/blob/main/docs/laboratory/experiments/EXP-20261116-private-credit-epoch.md) establishes two confirmed native private credits and complete accounting of their shared epoch. It does not establish owner-history completeness, confidential debit/exit, mixed-action epochs or public-observer action unlinkability. A source-level design or accepted service request is not proof of settlement; rely on the confirmed chain transaction and resulting state.

The detailed adversary assumptions and implementation evidence are in the [repository security documentation](https://github.com/bigdreamsweb3/trustlink-pay/tree/main/docs).

## Funding membership sets

The secret-derived nullifier removes the old public deposit-signature calculation, but privacy also depends on the eligible deposit set. In the November 5 reference run, the credit's public funding root exactly reconstructs from its sole index-zero deposit, and the public funding account contains one leaf. An observer can therefore link that deposit to the credit without recovering the secret or recipient TIN. Both historical nullifier controls reproduced; the updated nullifier differed from the former formula. See [the public-only evidence and method](https://github.com/bigdreamsweb3/trustlink-pay/blob/main/docs/laboratory/experiments/EXP-20261103-funded-note-credit.md#public-only-deposit-to-credit-regression-2026-11-05).

With several deposits, a shared root represents a membership set rather than identifying one consumed leaf. Public token/amount, timing and known prior spends can reduce that set. This investigation establishes a singleton link for the supplied pair; it does not establish multi-deposit unlinkability or retest recipient-registry correlations.

## Private credit amount and aggregate visibility

The experimental private-credit source uses fresh blinded amount commitments and leaves reserve liabilities unchanged on individual credit. Node-created, Receiver-coordinated and Cranker-submitted epoch work proves all 2–64 accepted credit openings. GPRU verifies the complete proof, and TCAP applies one aggregate liability reclassification. The first credit of each asset epoch can create one shared accounting account; subsequent credits reuse it, and accounted accounts remain on chain. Node considers 2–63 credits after 120 slots from the first credit, or 64 immediately; a singleton remains open. This timing is scheduling maturity rather than expiry, and the current transcript is credit-only. See [the TCAP lifecycle](/layers/tcap#native-credit-epoch-lifecycle).

Actual credit amount is absent from instruction fields and the old unblinded guessing formulas, but public epoch totals and known candidate amounts can still reveal values by inference. The November 16 execution evidence does not establish unlinkability of those real TCAP actions, varied-amount public-observer privacy or an independent security review. See [the decision](https://github.com/bigdreamsweb3/trustlink-pay/blob/main/docs/laboratory/decisions/ADR-005-private-amount-and-adaptive-pools.md).

Retained exit payout source publishes `destination_owner` and a destination token account. It is a separate account/instruction family, not evidence of an unlinkable private exit. A future exit path that preserves unlinkability between TCAP actions needs its own contract and execution analysis.
