---
title: "Protocol Responsibilities"
description: "A clear division of responsibility between transfer identity, private-state protection, and asset settlement."
---

TSN separates identity, private-state validation, and custody-related settlement checks so that a single component does not decide every part of a payment.

| Layer | Responsibility | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TIP | Manages transfer identities, including TINs, and their routing information. | A TIN is an identity, not a wallet key or a balance account. |
| GPRU | Guards the integrity and privacy of the user's private state presented to TCAP. | A proof does not grant custody or independently authorize payment. |
| TCAP | Governs accepted assets and liquidity, then applies authorized balance changes. | TCAP is the asset and settlement authority. |
| TSNP | Enforces the on-chain protocol rules for authorized settlement. | An instruction only has the guarantees its checks establish. |
| TSN services | Coordinate request intake, verification, and transaction delivery. | Service status is distinct from an on-chain result. |

For a balance-changing action, GPRU is intended to prove that the hidden state is current and that the proposed transition follows from it. TCAP checks the applicable asset, authorization, and liquidity conditions, then applies the authorized transition atomically. This keeps state protection separate from control of funds.

The experimental native-credit statement uses a secret-derived funded-note nullifier and a freshly blinded amount commitment. It omits the plaintext amount, note opening and private route/predecessor witness; individual credit updates shared head/root and epoch transcript without changing reserve liabilities. Public deposits, epoch totals, assets, roots, successor points, accounts, ciphertext and timing can still correlate payments. Node knows private openings and routes, and recipient-registry/head linkage remains unresolved. Matching deployed binaries/keys and live multiwallet accounting remain unverified; see [the privacy limits](/security/threat-model).
