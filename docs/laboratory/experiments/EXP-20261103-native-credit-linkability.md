## Execution and privacy are separate claims

The finalized credit accepted the proof relation implemented by GPRU, included the bound encrypted owner record in its Memo, and committed TCAP's root/liability update atomically. The programs have no additional predicate requiring public funding or recipient information to be uncorrelatable. An accepted proof therefore establishes the encoded checks, rather than the broader intended privacy goal. Owner decryption was not performed.

The shared TCAP program, reserve and root accounts alone cannot identify which deposit funded a credit or whether credits share a recipient. The separate deposit-derived nullifier provides an exact public funding relationship: Node hashes the fixed domain, TCAP program bytes and public deposit-signature bytes, then reduces the hash to the proof field. The baseline recomputation matched `079eea418f77cc6378099aae819780b7fa5b2265d678b022ce4e24e8ae40366b`. This calculation itself does not identify a recipient.

## Repeated credits and portfolio carryover

The [offline checker](../../../scripts/check-native-credit-linkability.py) ran fourteen assertions using synthetic fixtures produced by the current witness builder. An observer starting from known empty maps and maintaining inferred public history identified the same synthetic registry on a second same-token credit and identified a different registry in a control. Incorrect registry candidates failed. No private sequence was copied into the observer; its sequence followed the hypothesized history.

The public successor portfolio root supplies another correlation channel. It is a sparse tree of `Poseidon(tokenId, balanceX, balanceY)` leaves, while a credit updates only its selected token and preserves other leaves. Combining an earlier public point for one asset with a later public point for another reproduced the same synthetic recipient's portfolio root. Wrong-recipient and omitted-asset controls failed. This requires known or enumerable remaining portfolio points. A one-token portfolio root alone has no recipient-key input and is not a stable recipient identifier; its point/root rotated on the next same-token credit. The [offline result](../../references/native-credit-linkability-20261102.json) retains checks, source hashes and limits without publishing synthetic witnesses or secret scalars.

## Future one-transaction and unlinking preferences

An atomic deposit → authorized credit → Memo sequence is plausible at the program level: deposit increases pending liabilities, credit checks them, and GPRU requires the following Memo without forbidding an earlier deposit. The existing services require a confirmed prior deposit, a one-instruction wallet funding message and a separate two-signer credit/Memo message. Their nullifier depends on the deposit transaction's final signature; including that credit in the same message would make the signature depend on a proof that depends on that signature. This requires a funding identity established before signing and corresponding cross-layer authorization, rather than merely concatenating builders. Fees, wire capacity, compute, wallet approval and atomic execution have not been tested for that proposed flow.

One transaction would visibly associate sender funding with credit. Two transactions currently remain linked by the public nullifier and do not repair recipient-registry reconstruction. An unlinking option would need both an enforced private funding-spend relation and recipient-state commitments that cannot be reconstructed from enumerable public registry keys and public outputs. Hiding only the successor portfolio root is insufficient for the verified first-credit case because its single-token root is itself publicly reconstructible. Hidden high-entropy head/portfolio openings are a proposed direction that requires predecessor/successor, owner recovery, circuit, verifier-key and ABI review; no replacement design is adopted here.
