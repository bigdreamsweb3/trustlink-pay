# Participating-owner transfer preparation and recovery

**Status: HISTORICAL participating-owner prototype, verified at the scoped local-test level on 2026-11-23; superseded for operational design by the [offline-recipient transfer checkpoint](EXP-20261123-offline-recipient-transfer.md).** This preserves the original two-owner preparation/recovery experiment and its dated test results. The current replacement requires only the sender's opening/approval and credits the authenticated recipient point without recipient participation. Existing deposit credit is unchanged.

The source paths and npm commands below now describe or select the replacement. The original counts apply to the earlier participating-owner source/artifacts and do not verify the corrected sender-only circuit. Original `build/` and `build/dev/` artifacts remain historical evidence; current commands select `build/sender-only/` and its separate matching key, with no old-key fallback. Neither this record nor its former two-opening requirement directs current runtime integration.

## Problem and selected scope

The core requires both private balance openings to prove sender sufficiency and a bounded recipient successor. Existing credit can add to a recipient's Pedersen point without opening it, so the Node's public witness snapshot cannot supply those balances or blindings. Existing SDK credit-history recovery reconstructs openings internally, while its ordinary owner-balance response exposes display balances. The selected scope connects that existing owner recovery to local transfer preparation rather than assuming public commitments reveal private state.

SDK [`withTinOwnerTransferOpeningV1`](../../../tsn-protocol/sdks/tsn-sdk/src/tin-owner-balance.ts) validates the connected wallet and current TIP registry, unlocks the seed locally and recovers the selected asset opening from finalized supported credit history. It checks the expected shared root/global sequence before returning a packet to the caller callback. The ordinary display-balance response retains its contract. The callback receives no seed or reusable seed-access signature; its selected balance and blinding are reset on callback completion, although caller-retained copies remain the application's responsibility.

Both owners explicitly participate. Receiving while the recipient is offline and automatically acquiring recipient state are outside this contract. The adapter's per-owner registry hashes, routes and selected public snapshots are independent trusted caller inputs; a submitted packet cannot provide its own trusted owner pin.

## Opening consent and atomic preparation

[`transfer-opening.ts`](../../../tsn-protocol/sdks/tsn-sdk/src/gpru/tin-debit-opening.ts) recomputes the existing transfer amount, authorization, action and nullifier commitments. `buildTinTransferApprovalMessageV1` prepares the core's unchanged sender approval. `signTinTransferOpeningV1` requests a separate Ed25519 signature over `TSN_TIN_TRANSFER_OPENING_V1\0` plus canonical newline-delimited leg, authorization, roots/global sequence, full registry route-key commitment, asset, predecessor sequence/Memo, balance and blinding. It validates the signature and connected wallet again after approval.

The historical `participants.py` adapter rejected unsigned openings, supplied successor Memos and malformed packets. It validated both disclosure signatures against independent registry owner pins and bound their context to the selected routes/state. The core reconstructed each opening's balance point and authenticated both route and state paths. Sender payment approval remained separate from opening-disclosure consent. That retired adapter is replaced by the current sender-only [`sender.py`](../../../tsn-protocol/programs/gpru/circuits/hidden_head_transfer_v1/offline/sender.py); its present source does not implement this historical two-opening contract.

The adapter prepares successor points with in-memory placeholder Memo commitments, pins the same blinding deltas, constructs both encrypted records and rebuilds the final witness with the actual Memo hashes. Neither provisional candidate is persisted or accepted as chain state. A transfer remains one paired action with one replay identifier, one global-root update and zero custody/reserve-category deltas; the existing credit-only epoch transcript/circuit is unchanged.

## Encrypted records and independent recovery

Each `TSN_GPRU_TRANSFER_HEAD_V1` Memo identifies leg `0` for the sender debit or `1` for the recipient credit and contains the successor portfolio root/point, action commitment, encrypted blinding delta, public delta ephemeral point and a DBrix Ledger envelope. The strict encrypted payload uses `tsn-gpru-transfer-action-v1`, action kind `4`, leg, predecessor sequence/Memo and the private amount opening with its action/authorization/nullifier commitments. Existing credit/debit/exit record parsers are not widened into a transfer fallback.

Encryption reuses the existing Ristretto record key agreement with a fresh public ephemeral key, DBrix Secretbox envelope and BabyJub scan-key blinding-delta cipher. The raw record public key must match the TIP registry's exact versioned SHA-256 commitment before field reduction. No permanent Node key or new owner key is invented. Each complete Memo is hashed with the existing SHA-256-to-BN254 reduction into its leg's proof input.

[`recoverGpruTransferOwnerOpeningV1`](../../../tsn-protocol/sdks/tsn-sdk/src/gpru/tin-debit-record.ts) requires the exact independently verified twenty-scalar statement, the expected leg and an authenticated predecessor opening. It checks the full Memo hash and successor fields, decrypts under the registered owner keys, validates the transfer amount/action relations and predecessor chain, then reconstructs the updated balance point. Both positive blinding deltas are added modulo the subgroup; amount direction follows the leg. A full spend with zero resulting blinding may produce the valid identity balance point `(0,1)`.

The circuit binds Memo commitments into successor heads but does not prove correct encryption or record decryptability. The SDK helper verifies record bindings and arithmetic; its caller must separately verify Groth16 and authenticate the predecessor opening against the selected state. It does not verify a finalized receipt, discover live transfer history or confer settlement authority.
