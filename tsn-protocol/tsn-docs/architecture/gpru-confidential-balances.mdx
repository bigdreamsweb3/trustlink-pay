# GPRU and TCAP confidential state

GPRU (Guarded Privacy Routing Unit) creates transaction-specific DNA keys and verifies their relationship to TIP route material. TIP (Transfer Identity Protocol) manages transfer identities and route data; a TIN (Transfer Identity Number) is one identity within TIP. TCAP (Transfer Confidential Asset Protocol) governs approved assets and its credit state.

## Current implementation

TIP V1 stores a versioned GPRU ElGamal scan public key and its commitment. The SDK can recover a transaction-specific private key locally from the public DNA derivation data and owner-held secret material. The Node creates the DNA and one-time public key while the recipient is offline.

For TCAP credit settlement, TSN invokes GPRU to verify the DNA key derivation proof and encrypted credit transition, then invokes TCAP in the same transaction. GPRU verifies the first encrypted balance against the funded amount or verifies the homomorphic successor against the previous ciphertext and credit amount. TCAP checks authorization, asset and policy, validity window, predecessor commitment, and next sequence. On success, it atomically advances the replay head.

The Node derives a funding commitment from the signed reserve-deposit transaction. TSN includes it in the settlement authorization, and TCAP receives it in the transition's `nullifier` field. Once TCAP advances the predecessor and sequence, that same settlement cannot be applied again. This flow has no separate spent-funding record or per-transaction PDA.

The ElGamal balance ciphertext and encrypted transaction record are carried in the settlement transaction's DBrix Memo instruction. TCAP's `TCapTinTipV1` account stores only the current commitment, sequence, policy commitment, latest nullifier, and frozen flag; it does not store a balance ciphertext or transaction history. The owner recovers the matching GPRU key locally and reads the Memo history through an RPC/indexing service.

## Settlement sequence

```mermaid
sequenceDiagram
    participant Sender as Sender SDK
    participant Receiver
    participant Node as TSN Node
    participant Cranker
    participant TSN as TSN Program
    participant GPRU
    participant TCAP
    participant Owner as Recipient SDK

    Sender->>Receiver: Signed payment authorization and reserve funding
    Receiver->>Node: Accepted ingress work
    Node->>Node: Resolve TIN through TIP; validate route and funding
    Node->>Node: Derive DNA and one-time ElGamal public key
    Node->>Cranker: Immutable verified settlement authorization
    Cranker->>TSN: Submit exact co-signed transaction
    TSN->>GPRU: Verify DNA key derivation proof
    TSN->>TCAP: CPI with funding-bound transition
    TCAP->>TCAP: Check predecessor and sequence; advance state atomically
    Owner->>Owner: Recover the matching private key locally
```

Receiver acceptance, Node verification, Cranker submission, and chain confirmation are separate lifecycle stages. A confirmed reserve deposit does not by itself mean the later TCAP settlement transition confirmed.

## Debit boundary and privacy limits

Credit ciphertext updates use ElGamal addition without decrypting the stored balance. This does not prove that a balance can cover a debit. Confidential debit and exit remain fail-closed until the program verifies a proof that the prior balance is sufficient and the successor reflects the exact subtraction. TIN-to-TIN transfers also need a proof of source/destination conservation.

Wallet deposits and exits remain public at their wallet boundary. A transaction-specific DNA key does not hide those public funding details. The TCAP replay-head PDA is stable for its route and appears in credit settlements, so observers can link settlements that update that same head even though each transaction has a distinct DNA output.
