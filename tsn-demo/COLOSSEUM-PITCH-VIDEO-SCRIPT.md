# Colosseum founder presentation video

**Target runtime:** 2:25–2:45, never over three minutes. The pitch explains why TSN should exist and who is building it. Keep the detailed Receiver, Node, Cranker, and Solana sequence for the separate technical demo.

## On-camera script and shot plan

### 00:00–00:20 · Founders on camera

**Shot:** The founder or team looks into the camera. Put names and roles on screen. Do not open with a logo animation.

**Say:** “We’re [founder names], the team behind TrustLink and the Transfer Settlement Network. We’re building TSN because sending money with a long wallet address is hard for people to read, remember, and verify.”

**Fill in before recording:** Each founder’s real role and one relevant experience that explains why this team is positioned to work on identity, payments, or distributed systems.

### 00:20–00:48 · The problem and insight

**Shot:** Founder remains on screen, then cut to a simple wallet-address/TIN comparison graphic.

**Say:** “A crypto wallet address is useful to software, but it is a poor payment identity for people. A copied address can be difficult to check, and a mistake can send value to the wrong destination. TSN gives the user a private, ten-digit TIN to share as a more recognizable transfer identity, while the owner wallet remains in control of authorization and recovery.”

### 00:48–01:18 · Product in use

**Shot:** Screen share TSN XP. Show the Devnet network indicator, connect a wallet, create the identity, then reload and recover it with the same wallet. Keep a small camera view of the founder explaining what is happening.

**Say:** “This is TSN XP. The owner creates a TIN through the SDK and can recover it after reconnecting the same wallet. The identity is encrypted in the protocol flow, and the wallet signature for recovery only unlocks the owner’s record locally. It does not submit a transaction or move funds.”

### 01:18–01:45 · What is built

**Shot:** Show the SDK activity rail and the evidence view. Briefly show the Receiver ingress, signed Node verification, Cranker’s staged submission, and finalized Devnet transactions. Do not linger on code or raw encrypted data.

**Say:** “The creation flow is working on Devnet. The Receiver accepts the intent, the Node verifies and attests to it, and an authorized Cranker stages the registry write before final submission to Solana. Our captured run includes five staging transactions and the final registry transaction, all finalized. The technical demo walks through each boundary.”

### 01:45–02:12 · Customer and market

**Shot:** Return to the founders. Put a concise audience label on screen: wallet users, fintechs, and payment applications.

**Say:** “We’re starting with people and apps that need a human-readable way to address transfers. The SDK gives wallet and fintech teams a path to integrate identity-based transfers into their existing product. The universal TIN experience is what we’re demonstrating today. Institution-specific issuance is a direction we’re exploring, not a feature we’re claiming as shipped.”

### 02:12–02:38 · Why this team, validation, and next step

**Shot:** Founders on camera, then finish on TSN XP’s identity screen and the Devnet result.

**Say:** “We’ve built the SDK, the XP, the Receiver-to-Node verification path, and the staged Solana registry flow ourselves. [Add one honest sentence about user conversations, feedback, or testing; if there has been no external validation yet, say that clearly.] Our next step is to test the complete transfer experience with wallet and fintech partners, then use their feedback to shape integrations and issuer support. We’re [specific reason your team will keep building TSN after the hackathon].”

## Final checks

- Replace every bracketed cue with true names, roles, experience, feedback, and commitment. Do not invent users, pilots, traction, or institutional integrations.
- Keep this founder presentation separate from the technical demo. Colosseum asks for both, each under three minutes.
- Record the founder segments in one quiet take each. Capture the product screen separately, then edit to the speaker’s explanation with direct cuts and readable labels.
- End with the TrustLink/TSN XP name and project URL. Do not end on an unsupported promise or a roadmap feature presented as live.
