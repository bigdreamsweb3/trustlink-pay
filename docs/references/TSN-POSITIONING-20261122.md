## Positioning and source reconciliation

TSN is Transfer Settlement Network, the hybrid, decentralized settlement infrastructure being built for the Internet of Value. The ten-digit identifier is TIN: a blockchain-backed payment identity people share instead of a wallet address. Marketing presents the full intended experience of confidential value, identity payments, wallet payouts, connected settlement networks and owner-consented verified credentials, centered on privacy and recipient confidence. The pitch groups development progress on its own slide. Technical records separately explain TIP, TSNP, TCAP, GPRU, Receiver, Node and Cranker responsibilities and implementation/evidence limits.

Current experimental Solana credit requires an approved message, exact wallet funding approval, confirmed funded-note membership and a later independently prepared private transition. TSNP authorizes TCAP, which invokes GPRU before advancing shared state and the blinded credit transcript. Individual credit and complete aggregate accounting are distinct. The whitepaper and its Blogger copy now follow the current thirteen-input private-credit relation, secret-derived nullifier, shared roots and private amount commitment. Dated Devnet evidence remains scoped to its original observation; general unlinkability, complete owner recovery, debit/exit and cross-network delivery are not inferred.

The former destination hub, direct payout/executor, proof middleware, Node registry/payout models, Cranker EVM handler, dedicated funding helper and registry/payout SDK clients are removed. Generic EVM validation, Node route discovery and inert Solidity research remain. [Node route discovery](../../tsn-protocol/services/tsn-node/app/networking/cross_chain/ready_destination_networks.py) reports no enabled destination; [verification preflight](../../tsn-protocol/services/tsn-node/app/networking/cross_chain/verify_destination_route.py) rejects unsupported external routes before destination RPC, debit authorization or funding preparation. Initial HTTP/Receiver acceptance remains a separate asynchronous boundary. No old route is relabeled as a Base, Arbitrum or Polygon implementation.

Base, Arbitrum and Polygon are lower-fee evaluation priorities. Gas still applies; Polygon PoS is a sidechain, while Base and Arbitrum are Ethereum Layer 2 networks. Ethereum Layer 1 integration is outside the current scope. SDK/Node identity resolution currently returns owner-provided names and empty verified-credential lists. Signed credential-shaped fields do not authenticate an issuer. Optional attested names, business credentials and wallet-control claims require issuer verification, subject/TIN binding, expiry, revocation, consent and disclosure policy.
