**TIP (Transfer Identity Protocol)** manages transfer identities and their route information. A **TIN (Transfer Identity Number)** is one 10-digit identity within TIP. A TIN gives a sender a recipient identifier to resolve before authorizing a transfer; it is not a wallet key, token account, or balance container.

## Identity and wallet recovery

The TIP registry keeps identity information encrypted. The owner's recovery record is stored in a separate on-chain PDA owned by the TIN Registrar program, not inside the main TIN registry account. That recovery record contains the owner's public key and an encrypted TIN recovery envelope. When the owner reconnects the wallet, the SDK derives the recovery PDA, fetches the envelope, and decrypts it locally.

The registry omits a standalone raw owner-public-key field, but registration ownership is publicly recoverable: `owner_commitment` is unsalted SHA-256 of the registration owner key, the recovery account stores that key and derives its PDA from it, and creation data/Ed25519 owner approval expose it in transaction history. The Cranker payer can be a different key. Encrypting the ten-digit TIN therefore does not conceal the registry-to-owner-key association.

The native-credit ABI does not encode a plaintext recipient TIN or private predecessor pointer. It uses shared TIP/TCAP roots, and the owner retains private record lineage. The public-observer target is to hide TIN participation and prevent grouping later TCAP actions; trusted Node verification and public wallet funding are accepted boundaries. Registry/head dictionary correlations remain reproducible under known-genesis or maintained-history assumptions, while the two November 16 real credits were not matched to owners. See the [security boundary](/security/threat-model) for that distinction.

## Resolving a recipient

An application can use the TSN SDK to resolve the TIN's owner-provided display name and prepare a payment authorization. The display name is a recipient confirmation aid; it is not legal identity verification. The sending wallet still reviews and signs the payment authorization.

Native credit uses authenticated TIP route membership in its private proof. The [current capability record](/about/current-technology) links bounded Devnet execution evidence without making registry/head unlinkability or owner-history completeness claims. See the [SDK identity guide](/developers/private-tin-issuance) for application-level usage. Issuer operations and the registry's exact data contract belong in the [repository documentation](https://github.com/bigdreamsweb3/trustlink-pay/tree/main/docs).
