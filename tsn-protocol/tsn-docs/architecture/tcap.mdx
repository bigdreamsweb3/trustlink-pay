TCAP (Transfer Confidential Asset Protocol) governs approved assets, reserves, and liability accounting. In the current TSN wallet-to-TIN credit caller, TCAP also advances one shared commitment root after TSN and GPRU validations succeed.

## Current TIN credit state

The TSN instruction calls GPRU's DNA-key proof and encrypted-credit transition checks, then invokes TCAP `credit_gpru_memo_v1`. TCAP checks its canonical global commitment-root account, expected predecessor root and next global sequence, approved asset and policy, authorization signer, and validity window. It then updates that shared root atomically.

This is protocol-wide replay sequencing. The root is not TIP-rooted, is not a per-TIN balance account, and does not establish that a supplied predecessor ciphertext is the latest ciphertext for its TIN. The active credit caller does not initialize or update `TCapTinTipV1`.

The encrypted balance record is carried in the transaction's Memo instruction and bound by commitment. TCAP does not persist the Memo or balance ciphertext in a per-TIN account. A wallet or application that reads a TIN's history must retrieve and reconstruct relevant transactions through RPC or an indexer; the current source does not provide an authenticated per-TIN latest-head lookup.

## Funding-derived transition commitment

TSN derives a transition nullifier from the signed reserve-funding transaction and binds it into the authorization digest passed to TCAP. TCAP includes that value in the global-root transition. It is not a separate spent-funding account, and the global root does not prove per-TIN ciphertext freshness.

## Separate hidden-head source

GPRU and TCAP contain a Groth16 hidden-head verifier and a TCAP instruction that can invoke it before advancing the same shared root. The active Node, SDK, Cranker, and TSN credit caller do not invoke this path. The circuit does not prove balance arithmetic, debit affordability, exit destination binding, or reserve solvency. Its instruction ABI exposes action kind, token ID, and amount, and the common writable TCAP root remains visible in each transition.

## Legacy TIN-tip source

TCAP retains `TCapTinTipV1` and older initialize, credit, debit, exit, and liability instructions in source. Those instruction families are separate from the current TSN credit call; do not infer current XP account requirements from them.
