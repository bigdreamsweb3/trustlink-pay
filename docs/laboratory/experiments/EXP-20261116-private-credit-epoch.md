## Privacy scope and current-source controls

The privacy target is confidential TIN activity and unlinkability between TCAP actions for public observers. Public wallet funding in the mobile-money experience is an accepted boundary, and Node is intentionally trusted with routing and private openings. The accepted credit statement uses a freshly blinded amount commitment and a private-secret-derived funded-note nullifier. It omits the selected funding note, its index/opening and a plaintext individual amount. Public funding sets, deposit amounts, aggregate totals and known history can still narrow an observer's candidates; this checkpoint verifies execution of the private statement without establishing general unlinkability.

Fourteen synthetic structural checks were refreshed against the current witness builder with in-memory valid funded-note/membership arguments. All 14 assertions passed, including public registry dictionary and cross-asset portfolio reconstruction under known-genesis or maintained-history assumptions, plus wrong-candidate controls. The existing checker function was imported without its CLI main; a process-local absolute native-library setting selected the existing funded-note DLL. The old standalone CLI was not run unchanged: it lacks the current required funding arguments. These checks used synthetic identities, notes and snapshots; they performed no RPC, real-identity matching, Groth16 proving, decryption, signing or service operations.

Changing Memo keys, ciphertext and successor points does not establish a fresh unlinkable recipient identity: the current witness builder still uses persistent public TIP route material for the head key. Exact observer linkage of these two real credits was not tested. General source/recipient unlinkability, varied-amount multiwallet inference and complete owner recovery remain UNVERIFIED. Debit, transfer and exit inclusion belongs to the [proposed mixed-action contract](../decisions/ADR-005-private-amount-and-adaptive-pools.md#proposed-mixed-action-accounting).

Source inspection also identifies a public registration-owner association. TIP writes an unsalted SHA-256 hash of the registration owner public key into `TinV1Account.owner_commitment`, stores the raw key in its public owner-recovery account and derives that account from the same key. Direct/staged creation and the Ed25519 approval expose it in instruction data. Registry and recovery accounts appear together at creation; the Cranker fee payer is a separate key. This associates the registry with its registration authorization wallet, without revealing the ten-digit TIN, legal identity or every other wallet the owner uses. Under the structural check's known-history assumptions, a credit-to-registry correlation can compose with that association.

The retained exit SDK serializes `destinationOwner` and supplies the destination token account publicly. No accepted private TIN-to-TIN transfer or unlinkable future exit was verified by this checkpoint. Receiving through a TIN therefore defines the intended private interaction model, but current registration and exit contracts do not support an absolute promise that no observer can ever associate a wallet with that activity.
