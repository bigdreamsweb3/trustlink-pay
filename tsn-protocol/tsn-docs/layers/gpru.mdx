# GPRU: Guarded Privacy Routing Unit

GPRU derives a unique DNA and one-time ElGamal public key for a transaction output. The owner can recover the corresponding private key locally from the public derivation data and owner-held secret material. This avoids reusing the stable TIP scan key as the public receiving key for every output.

## Relationship to TIP and TIN

TIP (Transfer Identity Protocol) manages transfer identities and route metadata. A TIN (Transfer Identity Number) is one identity within TIP. TIP stores a versioned GPRU ElGamal scan public key and its commitment; the owner retains the matching secret material. Node resolves the TIN route and creates the transaction-specific DNA output while the recipient is offline.

## Current credit path

The TSN settlement instruction invokes GPRU to verify the DNA key derivation proof against the registered TIP route. If that verification and TSN authorization checks pass, TSN invokes TCAP in the same transaction. TCAP validates its asset and policy, predecessor commitment, sequence, and validity window, then advances its opaque state atomically.

The funding-derived commitment is bound into the settlement authorization and carried through the TCAP transition. TCAP's predecessor and sequence checks reject replay of the same settlement without a per-transaction account.

## Confidential balance boundary

GPRU key derivation and its on-chain proof establish that a one-time public key matches the route derivation. They do not prove that an encrypted balance is valid or sufficient for a debit. The current credit transition advances opaque commitments; it does not store an encrypted balance record. Confidential debit and exit remain disabled until the required balance-proof verifier is enabled.

GPRU does not hold token custody or receive the owner's long-term secret. TCAP remains responsible for governed reserve assets and commitment-state transitions.
