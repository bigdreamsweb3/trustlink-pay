Transfer Settlement Network (TSN) uses Solana as the chain for its current experimental settlement implementation. TSN is hybrid Internet of Value infrastructure: services resolve identities and coordinate authorized work off chain, while Solana programs enforce identity registration, accepted-asset funding, proof checks and state transitions. This integration is under development in the first release, v1; the evidence below concerns Solana Devnet.

## What Solana enforces

TIP manages the registry and shared route membership for a ten-digit Transfer Identity Number (TIN). TCAP governs accepted assets, reserve funding and confidential accounting. TSNP requires the applicable settlement authority before invoking TCAP, and TCAP invokes GPRU to verify the private transition and its encrypted owner record. Receiver, Node and Cranker have separate coordination, verification and submission responsibilities; none substitutes for the sender's wallet approval.

The current wallet-to-TIN flow requires a signed payment message followed by explicit approval of the exact wallet deposit. After the deposit confirms, Node prepares a funded-note membership proof and private credit transition. Cranker submits the Mother-authorized TSNP transaction with its proof-bound Memo. TCAP advances the shared state and credit-epoch transcript atomically; complete aggregate accounting is a later proof-verified action. See [how a transfer works](/overview/how-it-works) and [the payment lifecycle](/developers/payment-intent-lifecycle).

## What has been demonstrated

The [November 16, 2026 Devnet record](https://github.com/bigdreamsweb3/trustlink-pay/blob/main/docs/laboratory/experiments/EXP-20261116-private-credit-epoch.md) retains evidence for two finalized private credits and complete accounting of their shared epoch. This establishes that specific experiment, rather than general mainnet availability, an audited network or all intended payment routes. Owner-history completeness, confidential debit/exit and mixed-action accounting need separate evidence.

Private credit omits the plaintext recipient TIN and individual amount from its settlement instruction. Wallet funding amounts, aggregate epoch totals and transaction metadata remain public; Node intentionally receives verification routes and openings. These boundaries limit privacy claims, and confirmed execution does not prove public-observer unlinkability. Read [the threat model](/security/threat-model) before describing privacy to users.

## Integrating an application

Applications select their Solana cluster and TSN service endpoints, resolve the recipient, present the asset, amount, fee and expiry, and obtain both wallet approvals. They must reconcile funding and credit confirmations separately. Start with [SDK setup](/sdk/getting-started), [the payment guide](/sdk/payment-guide) and [readiness checks](/developers/network-status); endpoint reachability alone does not verify asset, reserve or proving prerequisites.

TSN XP is the reference application for the evolving protocol. These docs do not announce an app-store download, a production hosted payment service or a free-trial offer. Readers searching for a Transfer Settlement Network app download or free trial can use [current capabilities](/about/current-technology) and [the FAQ](/get-started/faq) to check the documented availability boundary.

## Cross-network and identity extensions

Low-fee EVM destinations and issuer-backed identity credentials are separate planned extensions. The Solana experiment does not demonstrate delivery to Base, Arbitrum or Polygon, nor integration with an external credential issuer. See [the cross-network roadmap](/overview/cross-network-roadmap) and [identity attestations](/overview/identity-attestations) for their scope and prerequisites.
