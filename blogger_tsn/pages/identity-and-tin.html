TIN: Ten-Digit Payment Identity for the Internet of Value

A TIN is a ten-digit Transfer Identity Number managed by TIP. It identifies a payment recipient while keeping user-facing identity distinct from settlement accounts.

Identity and routing

Transfer Settlement Network (TSN) is hybrid, decentralized settlement protocol infrastructure for the Internet of Value. TIP, the Transfer Identity Protocol, manages TIN registration, authenticated route membership and owner recovery records. The owner wallet authorizes its identity relationship; the Node resolves the permitted recipient route from current TIP state after the sender signs a TIN-targeted intent.

A TIN is not a private key, seed phrase or public token balance account. It does not authorize an arbitrary payment or remove the owner's separate approval of wallet funding.

From identity to private credit

In the experimental Solana credit flow, the sender approves a canonical intent and then the exact TCAP deposit. Receiver coordinates separate funding and settlement work. After funding confirmation, Node constructs the recipient witness and proof; Mother authorizes TSNP, Cranker submits the permitted transaction, and TCAP invokes GPRU to verify the hidden route and private balance transition. The result updates a shared root, rather than a public per-TIN balance account.

Optional verified credentials

A future attestation extension could associate an issuer-verified personal or business name with a TIN. It would require owner consent, a supported issuer and schema, correct identity binding, revocation and expiry checks, and selective disclosure. Solana Attestation Service or verification providers such as Sumsub are potential ecosystem references, not integrated TSN capabilities or announced TSN partners. Control of a wallet and registration of a name do not by themselves prove legal identity.

Privacy and evidence

Wallet funding and registration-owner relationships remain public boundaries. Node intentionally handles sensitive routes and witnesses. Owner recovery uses private owner material, but complete recovery and general unlinkability remain separate unverified outcomes. The retained November 16 Devnet checkpoint verifies two private credits and complete aggregate accounting; it does not establish confidential debit, exit or cross-network delivery.
